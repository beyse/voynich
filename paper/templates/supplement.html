S1. Parsing of the IVTFF transliteration

Page headers of the form <f1r> <! $Q=A $P=A $F=a $B=1 $I=T $L=A $H=1 ...> supply the metadata. Locus lines <f1r.1,@P0> text carry a locus type whose first letter, after stripping the position markers @ + * = ~ ! / &, gives the kind: P (paragraph text), L (label), C (circular), R (radial). Inline markup: [a:o] alternatives are resolved to the first reading; @nnn; extended glyph codes and ? mark a token as uncertain and such tokens are removed; <! ... > comments and {...} annotations are removed; <-> and <~> drawing intrusions are treated as spaces; <%> and <$> paragraph markers are removed; ! fillers are deleted; . is a space and , (uncertain space) is either joined (baseline) or treated as a space. The EVA alphabet is used as is; ligature and capitalized variants are kept as distinct glyphs. Resulting corpus: {{ v.lines_P }} paragraph-text lines on {{ v.pages_P }} pages, {{ v.tokens_P }} tokens after removing uncertain tokens; {{ v.labels_L }} label tokens.

S3. Generator parameters

Glyph automata. Counts of order-k contexts over the glyph stream of each line with k start symbols and an end symbol; sampling per line with the target glyph length of the corresponding real line, the end symbol suppressed until 60% of the target length and sampling stopped at 140%. Section drift: one model per (Currier language, illustration section) group with at least 30 lines, otherwise the global model. Page drift: the next-symbol distribution is λ times the page’s own normalized counts plus (1 − λ) times the section’s, for contexts seen on the page. Twins for the residual: order 3, section drift, fitted to the text under test.

Table and grille. Slot decomposition of each attested token by the regular expression prefix = q?(o|y|d|s|ch|sh)?(k|t|p|f|ck|ct|cp|cf|ch|sh|ckh|cth|cph|cfh|l|r|d|s)?, midfix = e{0,3}(o|a|y)?, suffix = (i{0,3}(n|r|l|m)|d|l|r|s|y|dy|ly|ry|dl|ol|al|or|ar|am|an)?y?, with unmatched tokens split into thirds. Per section group a table of 40 columns and max(2, round(0.9 × tokens/40)) rows, each cell an independent draw of prefix, midfix and suffix from the empirical frequencies. Each page starts at a random cell; a line is the run of cells to the right (wrapping); each line advances one row.

Copy and modify. Seeds: the 300 most frequent real tokens. Source choice: line above, same column ±1 (0.6); previous token in the line (0.2); random token of the page (0.15); seed (rest). Modification with probability 0.85; a second modification with probability 0.25. Modifications: substitution within the similarity pairs (ch,sh), (k,t), (t,p), (k,f), (e,ee), (ee,eee), (i,ii), (ii,iii), (a,o), (y,dy), (r,l), (n,m), (ol,or), (ar,al), (ain,aiin), (ey,edy) with probability 0.5; otherwise addition or removal of a prefix from {q,o,d,s,y,l,ch,sh} (0.25) or a suffix from {y,dy,n,in,iin,r,l,s,m} (0.25), the choice between addition and removal depending on the current length so that tokens stay around five to six glyphs.

Verbose cipher with fixed homophone choice. Each of 23 Latin letters is assigned one to three strings from a pool of 35 Voynich-like units; the homophone is selected by the code of the previous glyph modulo the number of homophones. Spaces are inserted after y, n, r, l, m, s once the current token has three glyphs, and forced at nine. One independent Latin sentence (split at . ; : ? !) per line, truncated to the required number of tokens.

Ciphertext-feedback substitution. ct = T[ct−1][pt] with 27 rows, each a random injective map from 23 letters to 27 glyph symbols; the two-glyph variant applies a second table to the same letter with the intermediate glyph as state. Spaces after the glyphs y, n, r, l, m at a minimum token length of three and a maximum of nine. Independent sentences per line.

Naibbe cipher. Tables from the file naibbe_tables.csv of the author’s repository (138 unigram strings, 138 prefix strings, 138 suffix strings for 23 letters and six tables). Preprocessing: lower case, w→uu, j→i, k→c, letters only. Tokenization of the plaintext into one- or two-letter tokens with unigram probability 17/36 (the last letter of a line is always a unigram). Deck: 20 alpha, 8 beta1, 8 beta2, 8 beta3, 4 gamma1, 4 gamma2, shuffled and dealt without replacement, reshuffled when exhausted; one card per plaintext letter (variant: one card per token). Two-letter tokens are prefix(first letter) + suffix(second letter). Post-processing: each ciphertext space is removed with probability 0.03 (variant: none). Plaintext: Latin (Caesar) or Italian (Dante); one independent sentence per line (variant: running text). State-dependent variant: with probability w the table of the next token is drawn from a fixed random distribution over the six tables conditioned on the last glyph of the previous token (one Dirichlet(c) draw per glyph), otherwise from the deck.

S4. Note on uncertainty estimation

A page-level bootstrap with replacement was tried first and rejected: duplicated pages create identical lines and token pairs, which inflates the plug-in mutual information (token MI interval 0.49–0.71 around a point estimate of 0.08) and depresses the hapax share. The leave-one-quire-out jackknife (Table 10) has no duplication and is the uncertainty measure used throughout. The jackknife standard errors of the excess-MI statistics include the variation of the estimator’s residual bias with sample size.
